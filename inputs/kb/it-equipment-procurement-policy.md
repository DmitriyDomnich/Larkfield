## 1. Standard equipment issued at joining

**IT1.1 Standard kit.** Every new employee receives a laptop, a phone and a headset from IT at no cost. The kit is ordered by IT and is not an expense, so the employee submits no claim for it. HR gives IT the start date at least 10 working days before the first day, so the kit is ready in time.

**IT1.2 Laptop.** The standard laptop is a business laptop with a 14-inch screen, 16 GB of memory and a solid-state drive. IT installs the operating system, disk encryption, endpoint protection and the standard office tools before issue. Roles that need more memory or a larger screen, such as data engineering, receive a higher specification after a request through the IT portal.

**IT1.3 Phone.** The standard phone is a current-generation smartphone with a company mobile plan. It is meant for calls, authentication codes and work messaging. It is enrolled in device management before issue, which lets IT lock or wipe it if it is lost.

**IT1.4 Headset.** The standard headset is a wired over-ear USB headset with a microphone. It is chosen for call quality and costs the company far less than EUR 300. An employee who prefers a different headset may buy one under clause IT4.1.

**IT1.5 Delivery.** Employees who work in an office collect the kit from IT on the first day. Remote employees receive it by courier at least 5 working days before the start date, and IT sends the first-login instructions separately by email to the employee's personal address. If the kit has not arrived 2 working days before the start date, the employee or the manager raises a request in the IT portal.

**IT1.6 Replacement cycle.** Laptops are replaced every 3 years. Phones are replaced every 4 years. Headsets are replaced only when they stop working. IT starts the replacement automatically and tells the employee 30 days before it happens, so the employee does not need to request it.

## 2. Requesting additional equipment through the IT portal

**IT2.1 The IT portal.** All requests for equipment that is not part of the standard kit are made in the IT portal, under "Request equipment". The request states what is needed, why, and the delivery address. Requests sent by email or chat are not tracked and are answered with a link to the portal.

**IT2.2 Approval.** The employee's manager approves the business need in the portal. IT then checks stock and either allocates a device from stock or orders a new one. IT does not approve a request that the manager has not approved.

**IT2.3 Delivery times.** IT delivers items it has in stock within 5 working days of the manager's approval. Items that IT must order are delivered within 15 working days. If an item is urgent, the request says so and the manager adds the reason; IT does not guarantee a shorter time, but it will say what is possible.

**IT2.4 Accessories.** IT keeps common cables, adapters and chargers in stock, and employees may request them in the portal at no cost. An employee may instead buy a small accessory personally. The Expense Policy (clause 4.3) reimburses accessories up to EUR 50 per claim, and a claim above that amount is rejected as a whole under Expense Policy clause 7.1.

**IT2.5 Laptops and phones are not bought personally.** IT supplies laptops and phones, and employees do not buy them. If an employee buys one personally, Finance can reimburse it only when a pre-approval reference was issued before the purchase, at any price (Expense Policy clause 4.1). This is allowed only in exceptional cases, for example when a laptop is lost during travel and IT cannot deliver a replacement in time.

## 3. Pre-approval references

**IT3.1 What a reference is.** A pre-approval reference is an identifier of the form `PA-YYYY-NNN`, for example `PA-2026-014`. Finance issues it before the purchase, and it shows that the purchase and its budget were agreed in advance. YYYY is the year in which the reference was issued and NNN is a running number within that year.

**IT3.2 Requesting a reference.** The employee requests a reference in the IT portal, under "Request pre-approval". The request names the item or event, the supplier or venue, the expected cost in euros, the date of the purchase, and the business purpose. Requests for equipment, team meals and conferences use the same form.

**IT3.3 Who approves.** The employee's manager first confirms the business need in the portal. A Finance reviewer then checks the budget and issues the reference, normally within 3 working days of the manager's approval. IT does not issue references; it forwards the request to Finance.

**IT3.4 Validity of 90 days.** A reference is valid for 90 days from the day it is issued, and the purchase must be made inside that period. A reference covers one purchase. If the purchase is not made in time, the employee requests a new reference; Finance does not extend an old one. The 90 days limit when the purchase is made. They do not change the 60-day claim deadline in Expense Policy clause 1.2, which runs from the document date.

**IT3.5 When a reference is needed.** A reference is needed for office equipment above EUR 300 per item (Expense Policy clause 4.1), for laptops and phones at any price (clause 4.1), for team meals (clause 2.4) and for conference tickets (clause 5.2). Team meals and conferences use the same reference as equipment, with the same 90-day validity. A reference is not needed for client entertainment, for office equipment up to EUR 300 per item, or for accessories up to EUR 50 per claim.

**IT3.6 Using a reference in a claim.** The employee enters the reference in the pre-approval field when submitting the claim. If a reference is required and the field is empty, the reviewer asks the employee for it under Expense Policy clause 7.2. A reference that was issued after the purchase, or that had expired on the purchase date, does not meet the requirement.

## 4. Home-office equipment

**IT4.1 What employees may buy.** Employees may buy monitors, keyboards, headsets and chairs themselves and claim them under the Expense Policy. Employees who prefer IT-supplied items can still request them in the portal at no cost. For other office items, such as a desk or a webcam, the employee asks IT first, because Finance assesses them under clause 4.1 of the Expense Policy.

**IT4.2 Limit of EUR 300 per item.** An item up to EUR 300 is reimbursed without a pre-approval reference (Expense Policy clause 4.1). The limit applies to each item and not to the whole order, so a EUR 250 monitor and a EUR 120 keyboard bought together are two items within the limit. The price counts after any discount and includes tax.

**IT4.3 Items above EUR 300.** An item above EUR 300 needs a pre-approval reference issued before the purchase. The employee requests it as described in section 3 and attaches the product page or quote. If the employee buys such an item without a reference, Finance cannot reimburse it.

**IT4.4 Receipts.** The claim needs the itemised receipt or invoice required by Expense Policy clause 1.3. The business purpose says what the item is used for, for example "second monitor for home office". The employee submits the claim within 60 days of the document date.

**IT4.5 Ownership.** Items reimbursed under this section become the property of Larkfield. IT lists them in the IT portal under "My equipment" so that they can be found and returned. At the end of employment IT normally allows the employee to keep a chair, and requests monitors, keyboards and headsets back under section 8.

## 5. Software and subscriptions

**IT5.1 Software is bought through IT.** Software, cloud services, browser extensions and subscriptions used for work are requested in the IT portal and bought by IT through the company account (Expense Policy clause 4.2). IT assigns a licence to the employee's work account. The employee does not enter a payment card.

**IT5.2 Why personal purchases are not reimbursed.** Software bought personally is not reimbursed, even when it is used for work (Expense Policy clause 4.2). IT cannot see, renew, move or cancel a personal licence, and it cannot check the vendor's data protection terms. A subscription that nobody at Larkfield owns also continues to charge after the employee leaves.

**IT5.3 Free trials.** An employee may use a free trial for up to 30 days to test whether a tool is suitable, but only after telling IT in the portal. The trial uses the employee's work email address, never a personal card, and never customer data or production data. If the team wants to keep the tool, the manager requests a licence before the trial ends, because a trial that converts to a paid plan without IT is a personal purchase.

**IT5.4 Open-source tools.** IT keeps a list of approved open-source tools that employees may install without a request. For a tool that is not on the list, the employee raises a request, and IT checks the licence terms, how active the maintainers are, and known security problems. Free software is still software for the purposes of this policy, and a tool is not exempt from review because it costs nothing.

**IT5.5 Cancelling licences.** The manager reviews the licences of the team every quarter, and IT cancels the ones nobody uses. When an employee leaves, IT removes their licences on the last working day. A licence cannot be moved to a personal account.

## 6. Security requirements for any device used for work

**IT6.1 Device protection.** Every device used for work must have full-disk encryption on, a screen lock that starts after at most 5 minutes of inactivity, and the endpoint protection agent that IT installed. Employees do not turn off or remove these controls. USB storage devices are blocked on Larkfield laptops unless IT gives an exception.

**IT6.2 Accounts and passwords.** Work accounts use multi-factor authentication. Employees store passwords in the company password manager and never reuse a work password elsewhere. Passwords are not shared, written down or sent by email or chat, and a suspected leak is reported under clause IT7.1.

**IT6.3 Personal devices.** A personal phone or computer may be used for email, chat and the authentication app only after IT has enrolled it in device management. IT can remove work data from an enrolled personal device, but it cannot see personal data. Customer data is never stored on a personal device, and a personal device that is not enrolled has no access to work accounts.

**IT6.4 Networks and public places.** Employees connect to the company VPN when they use public or hotel Wi-Fi. They do not leave a laptop or phone unattended in a car, a train or a public place, and they lock the screen whenever they step away. Work is not done on a screen that others can read, for example on a crowded train.

**IT6.5 Updates.** Operating system and application updates are installed within 14 days of release. IT may force an update after that time and restarts the device if necessary. A device that stays behind on updates may have its access to work systems suspended until it is current.

## 7. Loss, theft and damage

**IT7.1 Report within 24 hours.** An employee reports a lost or stolen device, or a suspected leak of credentials, to IT within 24 hours of noticing it. The report is made in the IT portal, or by telephone to the IT service desk when the portal is not reachable. The 24 hours run from when the employee noticed, but employees should not wait.

**IT7.2 What IT does.** On receiving the report IT locks the device and revokes its sessions, and it wipes the device if it is not recovered within 48 hours. For a theft, the employee also reports it to the police where it happened and gives IT the police reference number. IT keeps the report in the equipment record.

**IT7.3 Damage.** The employee reports damage in the IT portal with a short description and, where possible, a photo. IT repairs the device or replaces it from stock. Damage caused by accident is treated as a normal business cost. Repeated damage, or damage caused by ignoring the rules in section 6, is discussed between IT and the manager.

**IT7.4 Replacement.** IT replaces a lost, stolen or irreparably damaged device from stock, or orders a new one, after the manager confirms the circumstances. A replacement does not restart the replacement cycle in clause IT1.6, so a laptop that is lost in its second year is replaced and is still due for renewal in year 3 of the original issue. Larkfield does not deduct the cost of a device from salary.

## 8. Returning equipment when leaving

**IT8.1 Return deadline.** The employee returns all Larkfield equipment within 14 days of the last working day. IT sends a prepaid courier box and a return label to remote employees. The employee should not wait for the box if they can return the equipment to an office.

**IT8.2 What is returned.** The employee returns the laptop, the phone, the headset, the chargers and any item reimbursed under section 4, apart from the items that IT releases to the employee. The employee also returns access cards, security keys and the original packaging if they still have it. IT checks the items against the list in the IT portal under "My equipment".

**IT8.3 Data and accounts.** Before returning a device, the employee copies any personal files to a personal storage and does not delete work files. IT disables the work accounts on the last working day and wipes the returned devices afterwards. Personal data left on a returned device is not recovered after the wipe.

**IT8.4 Equipment not returned.** If equipment has not arrived 30 days after the last working day, IT informs HR and Finance. Larkfield may recover the replacement value of the missing items as the employment contract and local law allow. Items that arrive later are accepted and the recovery is cancelled.

## 9. Purchasing from suppliers above EUR 5,000

**IT9.1 Company purchasing is not an employee expense.** When Larkfield buys goods or services from a supplier, a purchase order is raised and the supplier invoice is paid by Finance by bank transfer. Employees do not pay a supplier personally and then claim it under the Expense Policy. This section applies to purchases for the company, such as equipment for a team, software contracts and consulting services.

**IT9.2 Team purchase orders up to EUR 3,000.** A team lead may approve a purchase order for the team up to EUR 3,000 within the team's budget. One written quote or a published price list is enough. A purchase order above EUR 3,000 needs the approval described in clause IT9.3.

**IT9.3 Above EUR 3,000 and up to EUR 5,000.** A purchase order above EUR 3,000 and up to EUR 5,000 is approved by the head of the department. One written quote is still enough at this level. The department head checks that the purchase fits the budget and that the same need is not already covered by a contract.

**IT9.4 Above EUR 5,000: three quotes.** A purchase above EUR 5,000 requires three written quotes from different suppliers. The requester compares them on price, delivery time, support and security, and records the reason for choosing one in the purchase order. When fewer than three suppliers can deliver the item, the requester records why, and the Head of Finance decides whether to accept fewer quotes.

**IT9.5 Purchase orders.** Every purchase above EUR 3,000 is placed by a purchase order issued by Procurement, which is part of Finance. The order states the supplier, the items, the price, the delivery date and the budget owner. The supplier must quote the purchase order number on its invoice, and Finance does not pay an invoice that has no number.

**IT9.6 Supplier checks.** Before the first order, Procurement checks the supplier's identity and bank details. A supplier that will handle personal data or customer data, or connect to Larkfield systems, also completes the security questionnaire that IT reviews. Only authorised signatories sign contracts, and an employee who is not authorised does not accept supplier terms on behalf of Larkfield.

**IT9.7 No splitting.** A purchase is not divided into several smaller orders in order to stay under EUR 3,000 or EUR 5,000. Procurement adds together orders that cover the same need with the same supplier within the same quarter, and applies the approval level of the total. Repeated splitting is reported to the Head of Finance.

## Appendix. Examples

- **Monitor within the limit.** An employee buys a monitor for EUR 280 for the home office and pays personally. The item is under EUR 300, so no pre-approval reference is needed (IT4.2, Expense Policy 4.1). The employee submits the itemised receipt within 60 days of the document date, with the business purpose.
- **Chair above the limit, expired reference.** An employee wants a chair that costs EUR 340. They request a reference on 10 June 2026 and receive `PA-2026-051`, which is valid until 8 September 2026. If they buy the chair on 12 September, the reference has expired and they must request a new one before buying (IT3.4). A purchase on 5 September would have been within the reference; the claim is still due within 60 days of the receipt date.
- **Team dinner.** A manager plans a dinner for a team of 12 with no clients and requests a reference through the IT portal. Finance issues the reference, and the manager pays for the dinner within 90 days and claims it with the reference (IT3.5, Expense Policy 2.4). Without the reference the claim would not be approved. Client entertainment does not use a reference.
- **Accessories and software.** An employee buys a USB-C cable for EUR 22 and an adapter for EUR 18 on one receipt. The total of EUR 40 is within the EUR 50 limit per claim (IT2.4, Expense Policy 4.3). The same employee also buys a PDF editor for EUR 40 a year with a personal card. It is not reimbursed, because software is bought by IT (IT5.2, Expense Policy 4.2); they should have requested it in the IT portal.
- **Team purchase order.** A team lead needs four monitors at EUR 900 each, EUR 3,600 in total, for a new project. The total is above EUR 3,000, so the department head approves, and one written quote is enough because the total is not above EUR 5,000 (IT9.3). Procurement issues the purchase order, and Finance pays the supplier directly. If the quote had been EUR 5,400, three quotes would be needed (IT9.4).
