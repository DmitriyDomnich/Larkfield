## 0. Definitions

**0.1 Employee.** Anyone on the Larkfield payroll, including probation.

**0.2 Claim.** One uploaded receipt or invoice together with the information the employee enters when submitting it (business purpose, attendees, pre-approval reference).

**0.3 Business trip.** Travel more than 50 km from the employee's usual office, approved by the employee's manager.

**0.4 High-cost city.** London, Paris, Zurich, Geneva, New York, Oslo.

**0.5 Pre-approval reference.** An identifier of the form `PA-YYYY-NNN` issued by Finance before the purchase.

**0.6 Reviewer.** A member of the Finance team who makes the final decision on a claim.

**0.7 Currency.** All amounts are in euros (EUR). Where a receipt is in another currency, Finance converts it at the European Central Bank rate on the document date; limits apply to the converted amount.

## 1. General rules

**1.1 Purpose.** Larkfield reimburses reasonable costs that an employee pays personally while doing their job. An expense is reasonable when a colleague in the same role would have spent a similar amount for the same business purpose.

**1.2 Submission deadline.** A claim must be submitted within 60 days of the document date. Claims submitted later are rejected. The deadline exists because Finance closes each quarter's accounts and late claims cannot be booked to the right period.

**1.3 Receipt requirements.** Every claim needs an itemised receipt or invoice that shows the vendor name, the document date, each item purchased, the total, and the tax amount where tax was charged. A payment card slip that shows only a total is accepted for amounts up to EUR 25. Above EUR 25 the employee must provide the itemised receipt.

**1.4 Duplicates.** The same expense may be claimed only once. Two claims with the same vendor, the same total and the same document date are treated as duplicates, and the later claim is rejected. If the employee genuinely made two identical purchases on the same day, they explain this in the business purpose.

**1.5 Business purpose.** Every claim states its business purpose in a sentence: what the expense was for and which project, client or trip it belongs to. "Work" or "business" alone is not a business purpose.

## 2. Meals

**2.1 Meals during business trips.** During a business trip, Larkfield reimburses meals at their actual cost up to these limits per meal: breakfast EUR 15, lunch EUR 25, dinner EUR 45. Limits apply to the employee's own meal. Meals on days without travel are not reimbursed unless clause 2.2 or 2.4 applies.

**2.2 Client entertainment.** A meal with clients or partners is reimbursed up to EUR 80 per attendee, counting every attendee including Larkfield employees. The claim must list the names and companies of all attendees and the business purpose of the meeting. A client entertainment claim without the attendee list cannot be assessed against the limit.

**2.3 Alcohol.** Alcohol is reimbursed only as part of client entertainment under clause 2.2, and within its per-attendee limit. Alcohol on a business trip meal without clients is not reimbursed.

**2.4 Team meals.** A meal for a Larkfield team (no clients) is reimbursed only with a pre-approval reference. Team meals are usually celebrations or offsites that managers plan in advance; the reference confirms the budget was agreed.

**2.5 Tips.** Tips are reimbursed up to 10% of the bill before the tip. Any tip above 10% is a personal expense.

## 3. Travel

**3.1 Air travel.** Flights are booked in economy class. Premium economy is allowed for flights over 6 hours. Business and first class are not reimbursed. Flights should be booked at least 14 days before departure; a later booking is allowed when the trip itself was arranged at short notice, and the business purpose says so.

**3.2 Rail travel.** Rail travel is booked in second class. First class is allowed for a single journey longer than 4 hours.

**3.3 Taxis and ride-hailing.** A taxi or ride-hailing trip is reimbursed when public transport is not a reasonable option: when carrying equipment, when no public transport runs, or when the trip starts between 22:00 and 06:00. The limit is EUR 60 per trip. Airport transfers in a high-cost city may exceed this limit when the receipt shows the airport as start or end of the trip.

**3.4 Private car.** Use of a private car is reimbursed at EUR 0.30 per kilometre. The claim states the start and end address and the distance. Fuel receipts are not reimbursed separately when mileage is claimed.

**3.5 Hotels.** Hotel rooms are reimbursed up to EUR 150 per night, or EUR 220 per night in a high-cost city. The limit covers the room and breakfast when breakfast is included in the rate. City taxes are reimbursed in addition to the limit.

**3.6 Hotel extras.** Minibar, in-room entertainment, spa and laundry are personal expenses. Laundry is reimbursed only on trips of 5 nights or longer.

## 4. Equipment and software

**4.1 Equipment.** Office equipment (monitors, keyboards, headsets, chairs) up to EUR 300 per item is reimbursed without pre-approval. An item above EUR 300 needs a pre-approval reference. Laptops and phones are supplied by IT and need a pre-approval reference at any price.

**4.2 Software.** Software and subscriptions are bought by IT through the company account so licences can be managed and cancelled. Software bought personally is not reimbursed, even when it is used for work.

**4.3 Accessories.** Small accessories (cables, adapters, phone cases) are reimbursed up to EUR 50 per claim.

## 5. Training and books

**5.1 Books and courses.** Books, online courses and certification exams related to the employee's role are reimbursed up to EUR 500 per calendar year.

**5.2 Conferences.** Conference tickets need a pre-approval reference. Travel to the conference follows section 3.

## 6. Not reimbursed

**6.1 Fines.** Parking tickets, speeding fines, and other penalties are never reimbursed, even when incurred on a business trip.

**6.2 Personal items.** Clothing, toiletries, personal subscriptions, and gifts for colleagues are personal expenses.

**6.3 Vouchers.** Gift cards, vouchers and prepaid cards are not reimbursed, because they cannot be matched to a business expense.

**6.4 Loyalty schemes.** Points or miles earned on business expenses belong to the employee; their value is not reimbursed or deducted.

## 7. Decisions

**7.1 Limits.** A claim that exceeds a limit in this policy is rejected as a whole. The employee may submit it again with the amount within the limit, explaining the difference in the business purpose. Finance does not part-approve claims, so that every booked amount matches a document.

**7.2 Missing information.** When a claim is missing information the employee can provide (attendee list, business purpose, pre-approval reference, an itemised receipt), the reviewer asks for it instead of rejecting. The claim continues once the employee answers.

**7.3 Unreadable documents.** When the document cannot be read (blurred, cut off, missing the total), the reviewer asks for a new photo or file.

**7.4 Personal items on a business receipt.** When a receipt mixes business and personal items, the claim is rejected under clause 7.1, and the employee submits it again without the personal items.

## Appendix A. Examples

- A dinner for EUR 52 during a trip to Hamburg exceeds the EUR 45 dinner limit (2.1) and is rejected (7.1). Submitting EUR 45 with a note is accepted.
- A lunch with two clients costing EUR 210 for three people is within the EUR 80 per attendee limit (2.2) when the attendee list is provided.
- A night in a Paris hotel at EUR 205 is within the high-cost city limit (3.5).
- A taxi from the office to a client at 14:00 for EUR 22, with no equipment carried, is not covered by 3.3 unless public transport does not run.
