## Definitions

- **Contractor**: an individual or company that provides services to Larkfield under a statement of work and is not on the Larkfield payroll.
- **Statement of work**: the signed agreement that describes the contractor's engagement, its fees and any expense terms. Abbreviated as SOW.
- **Engagement manager**: the Larkfield manager named in the statement of work as responsible for the engagement.
- **Expense line**: one receipt or invoice from a third party, listed on the contractor's monthly invoice together with the information the contractor enters for it (business purpose, attendees, written approval where required).
- **Business trip**: travel more than 50 km from the contractor's usual place of work, requested by the engagement manager.
- **High-cost city**: London, Paris, Zurich, Geneva, New York, Oslo.
- **Written approval**: an email or message from the engagement manager that names the expense or the budget and is sent before the purchase.
- **Reviewer**: a member of the Finance team who makes the final decision on an expense line.

All amounts are in euros (EUR). Where a receipt is in another currency, Finance converts it at the European Central Bank rate on the document date; limits apply to the converted amount.

## 1. General rules

**C1.1 Purpose.** Larkfield reimburses reasonable costs that a contractor pays personally while delivering the work in the statement of work. An expense is reasonable when another contractor in the same engagement would have spent a similar amount for the same business purpose. Costs of running the contractor's own business are covered by the contractor's fees and are not reimbursed.

**C1.2 Submission deadline.** An expense line must reach Finance on an invoice within 30 days of the document date. Lines that arrive later are rejected. Because contractors invoice monthly, an expense from early in a month can miss the deadline if it is held back for the next month's invoice. Finance closes each quarter's accounts, and late lines cannot be booked to the right period.

**C1.3 Receipt requirements.** Every expense line needs an itemised receipt or invoice that shows the vendor name, the document date, each item purchased, the total, and the tax amount where tax was charged. A payment card slip that shows only a total is accepted for amounts up to EUR 15. Above EUR 15 the contractor must provide the itemised receipt.

**C1.4 Duplicates.** The same expense may be invoiced only once. Two lines with the same vendor, the same total and the same document date are treated as duplicates, and the later line is rejected. If the contractor genuinely made two identical purchases on the same day, they explain this in the business purpose. A line already paid on an earlier invoice must not appear again.

**C1.5 Business purpose.** Every expense line states its business purpose in a sentence: what the expense was for and which part of the statement of work, client or trip it belongs to. "Work" or "project" alone is not a business purpose. The sentence lets the reviewer check that the expense belongs to this engagement and not to another client of the contractor.

**C1.6 Expenses outside the statement of work.** Only expenses related to the engagement named in the statement of work are reimbursed. Where the statement of work contains its own expense terms, those terms apply in addition to this policy, and a term that allows something this policy does not (for example training) is followed. A statement of work cannot raise a limit unless it names the limit.

**C1.7 Subcontractors.** This policy covers the contractor named in the statement of work. Costs of the contractor's own staff or subcontractors are reimbursed only when the statement of work names them, and they are bound by the same limits. Larkfield cannot check expenses of people it has no agreement with, so an unnamed subcontractor's receipts are rejected.

**C1.8 Change of status.** A contractor who later joins Larkfield as an employee follows the Expense Policy from the first day of employment. Expenses incurred before that day, while the contractor was engaged under a statement of work, stay under this policy and go on the last contractor invoice. Keeping the two periods apart means each expense is judged by the limits that applied when it was incurred.

## 2. Meals

**C2.1 Meals during business trips.** During a business trip, Larkfield reimburses meals at their actual cost up to these limits per meal: breakfast EUR 12, lunch EUR 20, dinner EUR 35. Limits apply to the contractor's own meal. Meals on days without travel are not reimbursed, because the contractor's fees cover their normal working day.

**C2.2 Client entertainment.** A meal with Larkfield clients is reimbursed only when the engagement manager approved it in writing before the meal. The limit is EUR 60 per attendee, counting every attendee including the contractor and Larkfield staff. The expense line must list the names and companies of all attendees, the business purpose of the meeting and the written approval. A line without the approval or the attendee list cannot be assessed and is not paid.

**C2.3 Alcohol.** Alcohol is never reimbursed to contractors. This applies to trip meals and to client entertainment alike, and the alcohol amount is not paid even when the rest of the meal is within a limit. When a receipt includes alcohol, clause C8.4 applies.

**C2.4 Team meals.** Meals with a Larkfield team, such as celebrations or offsites, are not reimbursed to contractors. When the engagement manager invites a contractor to such an event, Larkfield pays for the event directly. The contractor does not pay and claim the cost, so that the event budget stays with the manager who planned it.

**C2.5 Tips.** Tips are reimbursed up to 5% of the bill before the tip. Any tip above 5% is a personal expense. The tip must appear on the receipt or card slip so the reviewer can check the percentage.

**C2.6 Meals included in other costs.** A meal that is already paid through another cost is not invoiced a second time. This covers breakfast included in the hotel rate, meals served on a flight or train, and meals provided at a client site. When a contractor skips a meal that was included, no allowance is paid in its place, because this policy reimburses actual costs only.

## 3. Travel

**C3.1 Air travel.** Flights are booked in economy class only, regardless of the length of the flight. Premium economy, business and first class are not reimbursed. Flights should be booked at least 14 days before departure; a later booking is allowed when the engagement manager asked for the trip at short notice, and the business purpose says so.

**C3.2 Rail travel.** Rail travel is booked in second class only. First class is not reimbursed, including on long journeys. When only first class is available on a service, the contractor chooses a different service or explains the case to the engagement manager before booking.

**C3.3 Taxis and ride-hailing.** A taxi or ride-hailing trip is reimbursed when public transport is not a reasonable option: when carrying work equipment, when no public transport runs, or when the trip starts between 22:00 and 06:00. The limit is EUR 40 per trip. The limit applies to every trip, including airport transfers in a high-cost city.

**C3.4 Private car.** Use of a private car is reimbursed at EUR 0.25 per kilometre. The expense line states the start and end address and the distance. Fuel receipts are not reimbursed separately when mileage is claimed, because the mileage rate already covers fuel.

**C3.5 Hotels.** Hotel rooms are reimbursed up to EUR 130 per night, or EUR 190 per night in a high-cost city. The limit covers the room and breakfast when breakfast is included in the rate. City taxes are reimbursed in addition to the limit.

**C3.6 Hotel extras.** Minibar, in-room entertainment, spa and laundry are personal expenses. Laundry is reimbursed only on trips of 5 nights or longer. A hotel bill that mixes the room with these extras is handled under clause C8.4.

**C3.7 Local public transport.** Tickets for local buses, trams and metro during a business trip are reimbursed at their actual cost. A day or multi-day pass is reimbursed when it costs less than the single tickets the contractor would otherwise have bought. Public transport is the default choice for local travel, which is why taxis are limited in clause C3.3.

**C3.8 Changes and cancellations.** A fare or booking that the contractor changes or cancels is reimbursed only when the engagement manager asked for the change, or when the change was outside the contractor's control, such as a cancelled service. The business purpose states which of the two applies. A change made for the contractor's own convenience is a personal expense, and the cost is not reimbursed.

**C3.9 Parking and tolls.** Parking fees and road tolls paid during a business trip are reimbursed at their actual cost when the contractor attaches the receipt. They are claimed in addition to mileage under clause C3.4, because the mileage rate does not cover them. A parking ticket issued for an offence is a fine and is never reimbursed under clause C6.1.

## 4. Equipment and software

**C4.1 Equipment.** Equipment is never reimbursed to contractors. This covers monitors, keyboards, headsets, chairs, laptops and phones. Contractors supply their own equipment, and its cost is part of their fees. Where Larkfield needs a contractor to use Larkfield equipment, Larkfield supplies it directly through the engagement manager.

**C4.2 Software.** Software and subscriptions are never reimbursed to contractors, even when they are used only for the engagement. Where the engagement needs a Larkfield system, IT gives the contractor access to it, so that licences stay on the company account and can be cancelled when the engagement ends.

**C4.3 Accessories.** Accessories such as cables, adapters and phone cases are never reimbursed to contractors. The rule has no minimum amount, because small items are part of the contractor's own working costs.

## 5. Training and conferences

**C5.1 Books and courses.** Books, online courses and certification exams are not reimbursed to contractors, unless the statement of work says so. A statement of work that allows training names the amount or the course. Without that wording, the cost is the contractor's own investment in their skills.

**C5.2 Conferences.** Conference tickets are not reimbursed to contractors, unless the statement of work says so. When the statement of work does allow a conference, travel to the conference follows section 3 of this policy and the written approval of the engagement manager is attached to the expense line.

**C5.3 Training allowed by the statement of work.** When the statement of work allows training or a conference, the expense line quotes the clause of the statement of work that allows it. Finance checks the amount against the figure in the statement of work, and where the statement of work names no figure, against the written approval of the engagement manager. A line that quotes no clause is treated as training without permission and is rejected.

## 6. Not reimbursed

**C6.1 Fines.** Parking tickets, speeding fines, and other penalties are never reimbursed, even when incurred on a business trip.

**C6.2 Personal items.** Clothing, toiletries, personal subscriptions, and gifts for colleagues are personal expenses.

**C6.3 Vouchers.** Gift cards, vouchers and prepaid cards are not reimbursed, because they cannot be matched to a business expense.

**C6.4 Loyalty schemes.** Points or miles earned on business expenses belong to the contractor; their value is not reimbursed or deducted.

**C6.5 Business overheads.** Costs of running the contractor's own business are not reimbursed. Examples are office rent, internet and phone contracts, insurance, accountancy, bank charges and professional memberships. These costs exist whether or not Larkfield engages the contractor, and the contractor's fees are set to cover them.

## 7. Invoicing expenses

**C7.1 Monthly invoice.** Contractors invoice expenses once a month, together with their fees. Expenses are not submitted through the employee intake desk, which is for employee claims only and cannot process a contractor's expense line. A contractor who sends an expense to the intake desk is redirected to this process.

**C7.2 Separate expense lines.** Expenses appear on the invoice as separate lines, apart from the fee lines. Each expense line shows the document date, the vendor, the amount in EUR, the original amount and currency where different, and the business purpose. Fees and expenses are separate so that Finance can check expenses against this policy without reviewing the fee calculation.

**C7.3 Attached documents.** The receipt or invoice for each expense line is attached to the monthly invoice, in one file or several. The written approval of the engagement manager is attached where this policy requires it, for example for client entertainment. An expense line without its document is treated under clause C8.2.

**C7.4 Approval by the engagement manager.** The engagement manager approves the monthly invoice, including its expense lines, before it goes to Finance. The manager confirms that the expenses relate to the engagement. The manager does not decide whether an expense is within the limits of this policy; Finance does.

**C7.5 Tax and currency.** Expense lines show the tax amount where the vendor charged tax, in line with the receipt. The contractor does not add a handling fee or mark-up to an expense. Larkfield pays expenses at their actual cost, converted under the currency rule above, and any mark-up is removed.

**C7.6 Payment.** Approved expense lines are paid with the invoice, under the payment terms in the statement of work. Lines that Finance rejects are removed from the amount payable, and the rest of the invoice is paid on time. A rejected line does not delay payment of the fees.

**C7.7 Corrections after submission.** A contractor who finds a mistake in an expense line after sending the invoice tells Finance and the engagement manager before the invoice is paid. Finance then asks for a corrected invoice, or removes the line and lets the contractor invoice it again next month if the 30-day deadline is still met. Corrections made after payment are handled as a credit note against the next invoice, so that the totals on both sides stay equal to the documents.

## 8. Decisions

**C8.1 Limits.** An expense line that exceeds a limit in this policy is rejected as a whole. The contractor may invoice it again with the amount within the limit, explaining the difference in the business purpose, provided the document date is still within 30 days. Finance does not part-approve lines, so that every booked amount matches a document.

**C8.2 Missing information.** When an expense line is missing information the contractor can provide (attendee list, business purpose, written approval, an itemised receipt), the reviewer asks for it instead of rejecting. The line continues once the contractor answers. The 30-day deadline in clause C1.2 still applies to the corrected line.

**C8.3 Unreadable documents.** When the document cannot be read (blurred, cut off, missing the total), the reviewer asks for a new photo or file.

**C8.4 Personal items on a business receipt.** When a receipt mixes business and personal items, or includes alcohol or other items that are never reimbursed, the line is rejected under clause C8.1, and the contractor invoices it again without those items. This keeps each booked amount equal to a document total.

**C8.5 Disagreement.** A contractor who disagrees with a decision raises it with the engagement manager, who passes it to Finance with a short note. Finance reviews the decision against this policy and replies in writing with the clause it applied. The engagement manager cannot overrule a limit or an exclusion in this policy; only a statement of work that names the exception can do so.

## Appendix A. Examples

The examples apply contractor limits. Several would be accepted under the employee Expense Policy, which does not apply to contractors.

- A dinner for EUR 40 during a trip to Hamburg exceeds the EUR 35 dinner limit for contractors (C2.1) and is rejected (C8.1). The employee Expense Policy would allow it, but it does not apply here. Invoicing EUR 35 with a note is accepted.
- A client lunch for three people costing EUR 165 is EUR 55 per attendee, within the EUR 60 limit (C2.2), but only if the engagement manager approved it in writing beforehand and the attendee list is attached. If the lunch included a glass of wine each, the alcohol is never reimbursed (C2.3), and the line is rejected and invoiced again without it (C8.4).
- A taxi at 23:30 for EUR 38 is allowed under the night-time rule and is within the EUR 40 limit (C3.3). A taxi at the same time for EUR 46 is above the limit and is rejected, including when it is an airport transfer.
- A contractor buys a EUR 120 keyboard for use at a client site. Equipment is never reimbursed to contractors (C4.1), so the line is rejected, however small the amount.
- A contractor's three-night stay in London costs EUR 190, EUR 190 and EUR 195 per night. The first two nights are within the high-cost city limit (C3.5). The third is above it, so that night is rejected as a whole (C8.1) and may be invoiced again at EUR 190 with a note, while the other two nights are paid.
- A card slip of EUR 14 with no itemised receipt for a coffee on a trip day is accepted (C1.3) if it is attached to the monthly invoice within 30 days of its date (C1.2). A card slip of EUR 18 without an itemised receipt is not accepted, and the reviewer asks for the itemised receipt (C8.2).
