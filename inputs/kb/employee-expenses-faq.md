## Submitting a claim

**FAQ-1. How do I get back money I paid out of my own pocket for work?**

Submit a claim with the receipt and one sentence saying what the spend was for. Larkfield reimburses reasonable costs that an employee pays personally while doing their job, and "employee" includes anyone on the payroll, even during probation. A cost is reasonable when a colleague in the same role would have spent a similar amount for the same business purpose. (Expense Policy Definitions, 1.1, 1.3, 1.5)

**FAQ-2. Is it enough to write "work stuff" as the reason for the expense?**

No. Write one sentence that says what the expense was for and which project, client or trip it belongs to. "Work" or "business" on its own is not accepted as a business purpose. A good example is "Dinner with the client team during the Hamburg trip", because a reviewer can read it and know what the money bought. (Expense Policy 1.5)

**FAQ-3. How long do I have to file a claim?**

You have 60 days from the date printed on the receipt or invoice (the document date). Claims submitted later are rejected. The count starts at the date on the document, not the date you got home. The reason is that Finance closes each quarter's accounts, and a late claim cannot be booked to the right period. (Expense Policy 1.2)

**FAQ-4. I found a receipt from three months ago. Can I still send it in?**

Three months is longer than 60 days, so the claim would be rejected. The policy lists no exceptions to the deadline. If you think your case has a special reason, ask Finance before you submit rather than assuming it will be accepted. (Expense Policy 1.2)

**FAQ-5. I uploaded the same receipt twice by mistake. Is that a problem?**

The same expense may be claimed only once. Two claims with the same vendor, the same total and the same document date are treated as duplicates, and the later claim is rejected. Your first claim is not affected by this. You do not need to do anything about the first one. (Expense Policy 1.4)

**FAQ-6. I really did buy two identical things at the same shop on the same day. Will the system think I cheated?**

Two claims with the same vendor, total and document date look like duplicates, and the later one is rejected. If the two purchases were genuine, say so in the business purpose of the claim. That explanation is how the reviewer can tell the two claims apart. (Expense Policy 1.4)

**FAQ-7. I am not sure whether something is allowed. Who do I ask?**

Ask Finance, which owns the policy. Ask before you buy, because a pre-approval reference is issued before the purchase, and the policy has no way to get one afterwards. If the policy is silent on your situation, Finance is the team that answers. (Expense Policy Definitions, 1.1)

## Receipts

**FAQ-8. I lost the receipt. Can I still claim?**

Every claim needs an itemised receipt or invoice, and the policy has no rule that replaces a lost one. If you still have the payment card slip and the amount is EUR 25 or less, the slip is accepted. Above EUR 25 you must provide the itemised receipt. The policy does not say whether a bank or card statement can stand in, so ask Finance before you submit. (Expense Policy 1.3)

**FAQ-9. The shop only gave me the slip from the card machine. Is that enough?**

A payment card slip that shows only a total is accepted for amounts up to EUR 25. For anything above EUR 25 you need the itemised receipt or invoice. If the shop can print or email an itemised copy, ask for it at the till, because it is the safer document to upload. (Expense Policy 1.3)

**FAQ-10. What does a receipt actually have to show?**

It must show the vendor name, the document date, each item purchased, and the total. If tax was charged, the tax amount must be shown too. A receipt that shows only a total is not enough, except for the card slip case up to EUR 25. Check for these details before you leave the shop or close the booking page. (Expense Policy 1.3)

**FAQ-11. My receipt is in pounds. What amount do I claim?**

Claims are in euros, and Finance does the conversion for you. Finance converts at the European Central Bank rate on the document date. The limits in the policy apply to the converted amount, so check your spend against the limit in euros. (Expense Policy Definitions)

**FAQ-12. Finance sent my photo back because they could not read it. What now?**

When a document is blurred, cut off or missing the total, the reviewer asks you for a new photo or file instead of rejecting the claim. Upload a clear copy that shows the whole receipt, including the total. Lay the receipt flat in good light, and make sure the edges are inside the picture. (Expense Policy 7.3)

## Meals and entertainment

**FAQ-13. Can I grab a bite while I am away on a work trip, and how much can I spend?**

Yes, during a business trip. A business trip is travel more than 50 km from your usual office, approved by your manager. Meals are reimbursed at actual cost up to EUR 15 for breakfast, EUR 25 for lunch and EUR 45 for dinner. These limits apply to your own meal only. (Expense Policy 2.1, Definitions)

**FAQ-14. Can I claim lunch on a normal day at the office?**

No. Meals on days without travel are not reimbursed, unless the meal is client entertainment or a pre-approved team meal. Your usual office is the reference point, so a lunch near your desk is not a trip meal. Business trips need more than 50 km of distance and your manager's approval. (Expense Policy 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, Definitions)

**FAQ-15. How much can I spend when I take a client out for a meal?**

Up to EUR 80 per attendee, and every attendee counts, including Larkfield employees. For example, a lunch with two clients costing EUR 210 for three people is within the limit when the attendee list is provided. The claim must also give the business purpose of the meeting. (Expense Policy 2.2, Appendix A)

**FAQ-16. I took a client to dinner but did not write down who came. Is that a problem?**

Yes. A client entertainment claim must list the names and companies of all attendees, and without the list it cannot be assessed against the limit. This is information you can still provide, so the reviewer asks you for it instead of rejecting the claim. The claim continues once you answer. (Expense Policy 2.2, 7.2)

**FAQ-17. Can my partner's dinner go on the claim?**

The policy does not mention spouses, partners or other personal guests. Trip meals cover your own meal only, and a client entertainment claim lists the names and companies of the attendees you met for work. Every claim also needs a business purpose. Ask Finance before you include anyone who was not there for a work reason. (Expense Policy 1.5, 2.1, 2.2)

**FAQ-18. We split the bill at dinner with colleagues. Who claims it?**

The policy has no rule about splitting a bill. It does say the same expense may be claimed only once, so two people should not both claim the same receipt. For client entertainment, one claim lists all attendees. Ask Finance how to submit a split bill before you upload it. (Expense Policy 1.4, 2.1, 2.2)

**FAQ-19. Can I put a beer with my dinner on my trip expenses?**

Not when you ate without clients. Alcohol is reimbursed only as part of client entertainment. The policy does not say how to handle one receipt that has both food and alcohol on it. A claim that exceeds a rule is rejected as a whole, and mixed receipts are rejected too, so ask Finance how to submit it before you upload. (Expense Policy 2.3, 7.1, 7.4)

**FAQ-20. Can we have wine at a client dinner?**

Yes, alcohol is allowed as part of client entertainment. It must stay within the EUR 80 per attendee limit, together with the food and everything else on the bill. As always for client entertainment, the claim needs the attendee list and the business purpose. (Expense Policy 2.2, 2.3)

**FAQ-21. We want a team lunch to celebrate a launch. Can I expense it?**

Only with a pre-approval reference. Finance issues the reference, in the form `PA-YYYY-NNN`, before the purchase, and you enter it on the claim. The reason is that the reference confirms the budget was agreed in advance. The policy gives no way to get a reference after you have already paid, so if that happened, ask Finance. (Expense Policy 2.4, 7.2, Definitions)

**FAQ-22. How much can I tip?**

Tips are reimbursed up to 10% of the bill before the tip. Anything above 10% is a personal expense. The policy does not say whether the tip counts toward the per-meal or per-attendee limits, so ask Finance if you are close to a limit. (Expense Policy 2.5)

## Travel

**FAQ-23. Can I take a taxi home after a late client dinner?**

Yes, when the trip starts between 22:00 and 06:00, because public transport is then not treated as a reasonable option. The limit is EUR 60 per trip. The dinner itself is a separate claim that needs its own attendee list. Keep the taxi receipt, because it shows the time the trip started. (Expense Policy 3.3, 2.2)

**FAQ-24. Can I take a cab to a client meeting at 2 in the afternoon?**

Only if public transport is not a reasonable option. The policy names three cases: you are carrying equipment, no public transport runs, or the trip starts between 22:00 and 06:00. A EUR 22 cab at 14:00 with no equipment is not covered unless public transport does not run. The limit is EUR 60 per trip. (Expense Policy 3.3, Appendix A)

**FAQ-25. My cab from the airport in London cost more than the usual cab limit. Is that allowed?**

It can be. The usual limit is EUR 60 per trip. An airport transfer in a high-cost city may exceed it when the receipt shows the airport as the start or end of the trip. London is a high-cost city, along with Paris, Zurich, Geneva, New York and Oslo. The policy does not give a higher figure, so ask Finance if the fare is large. (Expense Policy 3.3, Definitions)

**FAQ-26. Can I upgrade to business class on a long flight?**

No. Business and first class are not reimbursed. Flights are booked in economy, and premium economy is allowed for flights over 6 hours. On a shorter flight, premium economy is not covered either. If you pay for an upgrade yourself, it is your own cost. (Expense Policy 3.1)

**FAQ-27. The trip came up at the last minute, so I booked my flight only a few days ahead. Will that be refused?**

Flights should be booked at least 14 days before departure. A later booking is allowed when the trip itself was arranged at short notice, and your business purpose must say so. Write that in the claim. (Expense Policy 3.1, 1.5)

**FAQ-28. My flight got cancelled and I had to buy a new ticket. Is that covered?**

The policy has no rule for cancelled flights. The normal flight rules still apply: economy, or premium economy over 6 hours. A booking less than 14 days ahead is allowed when the trip was arranged at short notice, and the business purpose must say so. Explain what happened in the business purpose and ask Finance how to claim the new ticket. (Expense Policy 3.1, 1.5)

**FAQ-29. Can I go first class on the train?**

Rail travel is booked in second class. First class is allowed for a single journey longer than 4 hours. A trip made of two shorter journeys does not qualify just because the total is long, because the rule is about a single journey. (Expense Policy 3.2)

**FAQ-30. I drove my own car to a client site. What can I claim?**

Private car use is reimbursed at EUR 0.30 per kilometre. In the claim, state the start address, the end address and the distance. Fuel receipts are not reimbursed separately when you claim mileage, so do not upload a petrol receipt for the same drive. (Expense Policy 3.4)

**FAQ-31. Can I rent a car for a client visit?**

The policy does not mention rental cars. It covers the use of your own private car at EUR 0.30 per kilometre, and the general rule is that a cost must be reasonable for your role and purpose. Ask Finance before you book a rental so you know it will be accepted. (Expense Policy 1.1, 3.4)

## Hotels

**FAQ-32. What is the most I can spend on a hotel room?**

EUR 150 per night, or EUR 220 per night in a high-cost city: London, Paris, Zurich, Geneva, New York or Oslo. For example, a night in a Paris hotel at EUR 205 is within the limit. A claim over the limit is rejected as a whole, and you submit it again with an amount within the limit. (Expense Policy 3.5, 7.1, Appendix A)

**FAQ-33. Does the hotel breakfast count toward the limit? And the city tax?**

When breakfast is included in the room rate, the limit covers the room and the breakfast together. City taxes are reimbursed in addition to the limit, so they do not count toward it. The policy does not cover a hotel that bills breakfast as a separate item, so ask Finance in that case. (Expense Policy 3.5)

**FAQ-34. Can I claim the minibar or a film I watched in my room?**

No. Minibar, in-room entertainment, spa and laundry are personal expenses. If these are on the same hotel bill as the room, the bill is a mixed receipt: the claim is rejected, and you submit it again without the personal items. (Expense Policy 3.6, 7.4)

**FAQ-35. I am away for two weeks. Can I send my shirts to the hotel laundry?**

Yes, but only on trips of 5 nights or longer. On a shorter trip, laundry is a personal expense. Minibar, in-room entertainment and spa stay personal on any trip, however long. (Expense Policy 3.6)

## Equipment and software

**FAQ-36. Can I buy a monitor or a better chair myself and claim it?**

Office equipment such as monitors, keyboards, headsets and chairs is reimbursed up to EUR 300 per item without pre-approval. An item above EUR 300 needs a pre-approval reference from Finance before you buy it. (Expense Policy 4.1)

**FAQ-37. Can I buy a new laptop or phone myself?**

Laptops and phones are supplied by IT. If you buy one yourself, it needs a pre-approval reference at any price. Get the reference from Finance before the purchase. IT supplies the device, so ask IT first. (Expense Policy 4.1)

**FAQ-38. I paid for a software subscription that I use for work. Can I claim it?**

No. Software and subscriptions are bought by IT through the company account, so licences can be managed and cancelled. Software bought personally is not reimbursed, even when you use it for work. If you need a tool, ask IT to buy it. (Expense Policy 4.2)

**FAQ-39. What about a cable, an adapter or a phone case?**

Small accessories such as cables, adapters and phone cases are reimbursed up to EUR 50 per claim. Anything above EUR 50 in a single claim exceeds the limit, and the claim is rejected as a whole. (Expense Policy 4.3, 7.1)

## Training

**FAQ-40. Can I expense a book or an online course for my job?**

Yes. Books, online courses and certification exams related to your role are reimbursed up to EUR 500 per calendar year. A claim that goes over a limit is rejected as a whole. The policy does not describe how Finance tracks your yearly total, so ask Finance if you are unsure how much you have left. (Expense Policy 5.1, 7.1)

**FAQ-41. Can I buy a conference ticket and claim it?**

Conference tickets need a pre-approval reference. Finance issues it before the purchase, so get it first and enter it on the claim. (Expense Policy 5.2, Definitions)

**FAQ-42. What about the flight and hotel for the conference?**

Travel to the conference follows the travel section of the policy. That means economy flights (premium economy over 6 hours), second-class rail, and hotels up to EUR 150 per night or EUR 220 in a high-cost city. (Expense Policy 5.2, 3.1, 3.2, 3.5)

## What is never reimbursed

**FAQ-43. I got a parking ticket on a business trip. Can I claim it?**

No. Parking tickets, speeding fines and other penalties are never reimbursed, even when incurred on a business trip. This holds even if a tight meeting schedule caused the ticket. (Expense Policy 6.1)

**FAQ-44. Can I expense a leaving present for a colleague, or a gift for a client?**

A gift for a colleague is a personal expense. The policy says nothing about gifts for clients, so ask Finance before you buy one. Gift cards and vouchers are not reimbursed whoever they are for. (Expense Policy 6.2, 6.3)

**FAQ-45. Can I put a prepaid card or a shop voucher on my claim?**

No. Gift cards, vouchers and prepaid cards are not reimbursed, because they cannot be matched to a business expense. If you bought something real with the voucher, the policy does not cover that purchase through the voucher, so ask Finance. (Expense Policy 6.3)

**FAQ-46. I forgot my toothbrush and a clean shirt for the trip. Can I claim what I bought?**

No. Clothing and toiletries are personal expenses, even when you buy them on a business trip. (Expense Policy 6.2)

**FAQ-47. I earned airline miles on a business flight. Do I have to give them back?**

No. Points and miles earned on business expenses belong to you. Their value is neither reimbursed nor deducted. Your claim is for the money you spent, and the points you earned do not reduce it. (Expense Policy 6.4)

## Decisions and appeals

**FAQ-48. My claim says "needs info". What does that mean?**

The reviewer is missing something you can provide, such as the attendee list, the business purpose, a pre-approval reference or an itemised receipt. If the document could not be read, the reviewer asks for a new photo or file. It is not a rejection. The claim continues once you answer. (Expense Policy 7.2, 7.3)

**FAQ-49. My claim was rejected because it was over a limit. What now?**

Submit it again with the amount within the limit, and explain the difference in the business purpose. For example, a EUR 52 dinner on a trip is over the EUR 45 dinner limit and is rejected, while a claim for EUR 45 with a note is accepted. The policy describes no separate appeal. If you think the decision was wrong, ask Finance. The policy does not say that a new submission restarts the 60-day deadline, so do not wait. (Expense Policy 7.1, 1.2, Appendix A)

**FAQ-50. Can Finance just pay the part that is within the limit?**

No. Finance does not part-approve claims, so that every booked amount matches a document. A claim over a limit is rejected as a whole, and you submit it again with the amount within the limit. (Expense Policy 7.1)

**FAQ-51. My receipt has a few personal items mixed in with the work ones. What happens?**

The claim is rejected, and you submit it again without the personal items. Finance does not part-approve claims. (Expense Policy 7.4, 7.1)

**FAQ-52. How long does Finance take to review a claim?**

Finance aims for 5 working days. The policy itself sets no review time. If the reviewer asks you for information, the claim continues once you answer, so a quick answer keeps it moving. (Expense Policy 7.2)

## Using the intake desk

**FAQ-53. What do I have to fill in when I upload a receipt?**

A claim is one uploaded receipt or invoice plus the information you enter. Always enter the business purpose. Add the attendee list for client entertainment, and the pre-approval reference where the policy requires one: team meals, equipment above EUR 300, laptops and phones, and conference tickets. (Expense Policy Definitions, 1.5, 2.2, 2.4, 4.1, 5.2)

**FAQ-54. Can I upload several receipts from one trip as a single claim?**

A claim is one receipt or invoice with its information, so each receipt is its own claim. The policy does not describe bundling several receipts into one upload, so ask Finance if you want to try. Each claim states its own business purpose, which is also what separates genuine look-alike purchases from duplicates. (Expense Policy Definitions, 1.4, 1.5)

**FAQ-55. Who decides whether my claim is approved?**

A reviewer, who is a member of the Finance team, makes the final decision on a claim. The reviewer applies the Expense Policy, and where this FAQ differs from the policy, the policy decides. (Expense Policy Definitions, 7.1)
